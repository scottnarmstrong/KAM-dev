Per the orchestrator's ruling (W5 audit: `stepTheorem(Mod)` (v) bounds `Φ - id`, `DΦ - I` only in the ambient

## AUDIT FOCUS

1. **The dependency statements** `W6StepSmooth`, `W6StepSmoothMod` (`StepSmooth.lean`): they must be exactly what W5
   (`inp-w5aa`) provides — check the constants (`M_F` sharp sup bound, `8M_F/σ` for `DΨ`), the domain (`D_+`), and
   that they are about `H.hyp.core.Phi` (the objects of `stepTheorem(Mod)`). Nothing else W6 uses is assumed.
2. **Compositions on one carrier.** `w6PhiK_eq`, `w6PhiK_fderiv_apply` identify the `KamPhase` factor with W5's
   `Φ_{ν+1}` and its derivative. The recursion `Ψ^{ν+1} = Ψ_{ν+1} + Ψ^ν∘Φ_{ν+1}` gives `Φ^ν = id + ιΨ^ν`,
   `DΦ^ν = I + ι∘DΨ^ν`, `|DΨ^ν(w)W|_{r_0} ≤ (c_ν-1)|W|_{r_ν}`, `c_ν = ∏_{μ<ν}(1+2^{-μ-2}) ≤ 2` (per step
   `8M_{F,ν}/σ_ν ≤ 2^{-ν-3}`, `w6_eight_MF_le`, and `|·|_{r_ν} ≤ |·|_{r_{ν+1}}`). Both (iv) bounds follow.
3. **Cauchy step.** `w6PsiC_cauchy`: `|Ψ^{ν+1}(w) - Ψ^ν(w)|_{r_0} ≤ |Ψ_{ν+1}(w)|_{r_ν} + |Ψ^ν(Φ_{ν+1}w) - Ψ^ν(w)|_{r_0}
   ≤ M_F + M_F` (mean value on the convex `D_ν`, `kamNorm_sub_le_of_segment`); blueprint `2·2M_F`. Tails via
   W6-PREP's `cauchy_tail_le` (`≤ e_Φ 8^{-n}`).
4. **Weak conjugacy route** (`i-weak`): composition of W5's field-form weak conjugacy (`stepTheorem` (i) conjunct)
   along the chain (`w6StepK_weakConj`, `w6Compose_weak`), instead of differentiating `H_0∘Φ^ν = H_ν + Σc_μ`;
   same content (W5 proved that conjunct from the `n-conj` derivative formulas); no extension of `DΦ^ν` to `𝒫^{p-1}`.
5. **Limit** (`i-lim`, `w6InvarianceIt`): left side by continuity of `X_{H_0}` at `Φ_*θ ∈ D_0` (`stepXN`
   continuous, `X_{P_0}` holomorphic on `D_0`, `IsSmoothing.holoX`); right side by (iii)'s derivative convergence,
   `ω_ν → ω_*` (`w6ConvFreq` (ii)) and `|DΦ^ν(ιθ) ιX_{P_ν}|_{p,r_0} ≤ 2ε_ν ≤ 2ε_0 4^{-ν}`.
6. **Tori interface.** `w6ToriKam_of_stepSmooth` (iii) uses `bpW:w6:invariance` at `w = Φ_*θ`, the modified step's
   symplecticity/onto/field weak conjugacy (`stepTheoremMod` (i), `prepK_clauses`), `w6StrongConjK`, chain rule.
