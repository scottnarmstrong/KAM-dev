# Errata and deviations

> **Writer's draft, not yet verified.** Every erratum below is a *candidate*: none has yet been checked against the pinned PDF by an independent refute-first verifier, and none is a claim that the authors made an error until that check confirms it. Entries are marked with their status in the findings ledger [`errata/ledger.json`](errata/ledger.json); no status has been upgraded here. The bracketed ledger identifiers are kept for the verifier and are removed at finalization.

This record lists where the Lean statements and proofs of this formalization differ from the printed sources, and why. An **erratum** is a defect in a pinned source itself: a typo, a wrong constant or sign, a missing hypothesis, a statement false as printed, or a gap. **Gap policy:** a gap counts as an erratum only when a printed assertion is false; where we supply a missing argument for a true step, the entry is a deviation (a proof supplement). A **deviation** is a deliberate difference of a formal statement or proof from its source: scope, conventions (including Lean's total functions, such as `x / 0 = 0`), the reading of an ambiguous phrase, strengthened, specialized or reformulated statements, explicit parameter windows in place of "small enough", proof supplements, and different proof routes. Repairs of the project's own written proof are neither, and are listed separately at the end.

Locations refer to the pinned version of each source recorded in [`sources.json`](sources.json) (arXiv version or DOI, with the SHA-256 of the pinned PDF); where printed and arXiv numbering differ, the entry says which it uses. Counts are of entries, not occurrences; a grouped entry may cover repeated occurrences of one misprint, and editorial misprints are counted.

**Effect on the main results.** No candidate erratum changes a frozen statement silently: every frozen theorem is stated against the corrected reading, and where a source statement needed a correction the difference is listed as a deviation with its effect. All 22 frozen theorems (listed in [`STATEMENTS.md`](STATEMENTS.md)) are proved using only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Pos01

### Errata

4. **p. 6, after the definition of Ω_α.** *false as stated; candidate erratum, not yet verified.* Printed: "or at least of dimension n − 1 so that the measure of a boundary layer of size α is O(α)". ## Formalization conventions

These are reviewed conventions of the formalization, not differences from any one source. Each is enforced by a guard at every consumer, so no theorem relies on a value outside the stated domain.

1. **Totality of partial operations.** Lean's division, inverse, supremum, and choice are total. Where a source object is defined only under a condition (an attained supremum, a nonempty compact interval, an invertible matrix, a nonzero denominator), the Lean definition returns a default value off that condition, and every theorem that uses it assumes or proves the condition. This applies to the averaging remark (attained supremum, nonempty compact interval, frequency of class C²) as well.
2. **Order of coordinates.** Points of phase space are written in the order (position, momentum) = (angle, action); where a source writes (action, angle), the relabelling is applied before its statements are used. It introduces no sign change.
3. **Weighted norms.** The weighted ℓ¹ norm of a source is [0, ∞]-valued. The Lean interface states summability and a finite weighted bound as separate predicates, and the real-valued norm is used only under them (it is 0 off the summable locus).
4. **The Kepler solver.** The Delaunay change of coordinates is faithful on its domain of definition; the value of the totalized solver outside that domain is not claimed to be the source map.
5. **Choice-based data in the secular normal form.** Data that the source asserts to exist are selected by choice with a default fallback, each with a proved local specification; the exact polar shell and the complex pullback identifications are formalized as separate objects.

## Repairs of our own written proof

These are corrections to the project's own intermediate statements, found by independent review before the corresponding steps were proved. They are not errata of any source and change no frozen statement.

1. **Nekhoroshev stability, frequency bounds of the constant-frequency steps.** Three steps (the exceptional set, the time bound and their conclusion) stated frequency bounds without the hypotheses on the unperturbed Hamiltonian, which the written proof bound only in a later clause; without them the steps are false (take h = 0). The hypotheses were added to the three steps; the Lean proofs `const_exceptional`, `const_time` and `const_conclusion` assume them, and the frozen theorems supply them.
2. **Properly degenerate KAM, analytic extension of the perturbation.** The written proof claimed that the extension agrees with the perturbation at all real points of the complex domain; this is false. Agreement holds on the closed real cylinder, which is all that any consumer uses, and that is what the Lean proves.
3. **Planetary preparation, scope of constants.** The constants of the preparation step may depend on the orders of the derivatives considered and are chosen before ε; the Lean statements quantify them in this order.
4. **Secular torsion, cut to its first clause.** A step that also listed later assembly tasks was cut to its claim (the minimum of |det β̂| over the compact set is positive); the listed tasks are proved where they are used.
5. **Secular cutoff loss and real-Lipschitz bound.** The cutoff-loss addendum needs d ≤ 1 and T ≥ 1, and the real-Lipschitz coefficient bound needs M₁ ≥ 0 (without it the second inequality can fail when no nonzero mode has order at most T); these premises were added, and the degree-zero case is treated separately.
6. **Dimension n ≥ 1 in the Fourier analysis layer.** Several analysis statements of the written proof were stated for all n and needed n ≥ 1; the premise was added.

## How the formal proof route differs from the sources

The positive-measure KAM theorem is proved from an a-posteriori fixed-frequency KAM theorem in the parameterization style (no canonical changes of variables), applied at each Diophantine frequency, rather than by Pöschel's iteration of symplectic transformations; Lipschitz dependence on the frequency is obtained by re-applying the a-posteriori theorem, and the measure step uses a McShane extension. The measure rate obtained is ε^{1/4}, not O(√ε). Local uniqueness of invariant tori of flows has no published statement in this framework; it is proved here by adapting the argument for maps. The per-source deviation lists give the other route differences.
